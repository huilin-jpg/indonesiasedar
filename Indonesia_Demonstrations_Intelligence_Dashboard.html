Public demonstrations in Indonesia: drivers, actors, framing, escalation and brand exposure

60-day core assessment (5 Sep – 4 Nov 2026, WIB) plus a 90-day weekly lead-in from 3 Aug with a focus on the 18 Oct – 4 Nov escalation around Prabowo–Gibran's second anniversary and the 28 Oct Youth Pledge Day #IndonesiaSadar mobilisation. Special lens: PT Astra International Tbk and its businesses.

Transparency: AI-assisted analysis of Meltwater listening data plus cited public reporting. It requires human verification before operational or security use. Ratings are analytical judgements, not predictions; peaceful protest and criticism are not treated as threats.

Executive read

What changed: a dispersed, haze-led protest wave (September–mid October) consolidated into a branded national movement, #IndonesiaSadar, convened by BEM UI and Komite SADAR. Online volume rose from ~3.4K mentions a day to 645K on 28 Oct, and street action peaked that evening in central Jakarta with tear gas, water cannon and 13–19 arrests. By 3 Nov volume had fallen 82% from the peak but was still ~35 times the September baseline, so the capacity to mobilise remains.

    ABC assessment

    Actors and affiliations

    Actors are classified by type and role. The identity basis is shown because a stance attributed to a group is only as reliable as the source that names it. Private individuals are described by role, not by name.

    Organiser core

    BEM UI and Komite SADAR convene. Trisakti, UT, UNS, Pakuan, Palangka Raya, UGM and ITB student blocs supply regional turnout. GMNI and HMI cadres lead in the regions (Samarinda).

    Legitimacy multipliers

    Mothers' groups, angkot drivers, faith communities, academics, celebrities (#ArtistBersuara) and the diaspora widen the base beyond students. They drive the highest-engagement content and complicate any "anarchist students" framing.

    State and counter-actors

    Polri and Brimob (≈9K personnel on 20 Oct, ≈14K on 28 Oct), Komdigi, regional governors, and Gerindra and official accounts. Counter-frames include "outsiders", "funded by conglomerates" and "foreign intervention".

    Media portrayal and legitimacy

    How each media layer frames the demonstrations, their tone, and the legitimacy it grants. The biggest shift happened after dusk on 28 Oct, when coverage moved from "orderly civic action" to "chaos" (see Binokular's analysis).

    Brands, institutions and the Astra lens

    Companies, platforms and institutions referenced in the protest corpus, by context and narrative. The Astra lens covers all 15 requested entities, including those with no mentions: absence of evidence within the sampled corpus is reported explicitly.

    Bottom line for Astra: No Astra entity is a target of the demonstrations. Two independent checks agree. (1) Of the 100 highest-engagement protest-corpus documents matching "Astra", 92 were keyword noise (motorsport "rally", OpenAI's "GPT-6 Astra", product "demonstrasi"). None of the 500 highest-engagement news, Instagram, Facebook and Reddit protest documents mentioned any Astra entity. (2) The entity-level audit in the pack (15 entities × 10 documents = 150 reviewed) found one incidental Astra International market mention and no direct targeting. Real exposure is concentrated in Astra Agro Lestari, through the haze and palm-oil narrative and community protests in Sulawesi, and in the physical proximity of Sudirman CBD assets to the Bundaran HI corridor.

      Limitations and analyst notes

      • Corpus noise. The boolean's "rally", "demo" and "protes" terms with country:ID also match motorsport, product demos, and Indonesian-language coverage of foreign protests (France, Milan, Serbia, Nepal, Vietnam). Volume totals are therefore an upper bound on Indonesia-protest conversation. Narrative and actor findings rest on reviewed documents.
      • X data. Text, handles and URLs are withheld under platform terms. Coordination analysis relies on content type, author IDs, source reach, named handles in enrichments and geo codes. Posts are referenced by tweet ID, not quoted.
      • Samples are not populations. Document pulls are relevance-, engagement- or date-sorted pages of 100. Shares in the coordination table describe those windows, not all of X.
      • Astra coverage is partial. Entity-level keyword pulls for the 15 Astra entities and a news pull for the peak window were blocked by the API rate limit (HTTP 429), and the Nexus boolean-count endpoint returned a permission error. Astra findings use the "Astra" keyword sample (n=100), a scan of 500 high-engagement protest documents, and public reporting. "No mention found" means none in these samples, not a verified zero.
      • Counts across calls. The hourly series (27–30 Oct) sums to 1.568M against a 1.570M window total because of index refresh between calls. Daily series are from single calls.
      • Arrest figures differ by source (LBH Jakarta: 13 incl. one child; BBC Indonesia and police: 19). Both are shown.
      • Geo. The schematic map uses approximate city coordinates. Site attribution comes from reviewed coverage, not from author geo-tags, which are mostly unresolved ("zz") or implausible (for example, Japan or Lebanon for Indonesian-language reposts).
      • Personal data: private individuals are referenced by role. Public officials, organisations and media outlets are named.
